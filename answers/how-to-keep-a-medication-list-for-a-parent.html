How do you keep a medication list for an aging parent?

Keep one written list, in one place, that every helper uses. For each medication, record the name, the dose, when it's taken, what it's for, and who prescribed it. Update the list the day anything changes, and bring it to every appointment.

What to record for each medication

  • Name, and the strength on the label
  • Dose and when it's taken (morning, midday, evening, bedtime, as needed)
  • What it was prescribed for, in plain words
  • Prescriber and pharmacy
  • Start date, and the date of any change
  • Notes from the label or the pharmacist: with food, not with something else, refill due

Keep allergies and reactions on the same page or the page next to it. A new provider or a pharmacist will ask for them.

Keeping it current

  • One list, not several. When the family, a sitter and a home-health aide each keep their own, they drift apart.
  • Log every change. Record what changed, who authorized it and when. A short change log beside the list makes the history clear.
  • Bring it to every visit. Ask the provider to review it, then write any change in the moment.
  • Check it against the bottles after a pharmacy visit or a hospital stay.

The list is a record. The prescriber and the pharmacist confirm every detail, and any change to a medication comes from them.
