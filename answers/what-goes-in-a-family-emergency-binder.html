What goes in a family emergency binder?

A family emergency binder holds the details someone else would need to step in for your household: who to call, the medical basics for each person, where the money and insurance live, and where the important papers are kept. It's one place, written down, instead of facts spread across phones, drawers and memory.

What to put in it

  • The people. Everyone in the household, plus dependents, with the basics a helper would ask first.
  • Contacts. The people to call first, the professionals who know your family (doctor, lawyer, accountant, insurance agent), and the wider circle to tell later.
  • Medical. A snapshot for each adult, their current medications, and where the care-preference documents are kept.
  • Money. Bank accounts, cards, loans, investments, retirement accounts, taxes, digital assets and recurring subscriptions. Record the institution and where to find the details, not full passwords.
  • Insurance. Every policy, with the insurer, policy number and who to call.
  • Document locations. Where the originals are: a drawer, a safe, a lawyer's office.

How to start

Start with the contacts and the medical page for each person. Those are the pages someone reaches for first. Fill in what you know tonight and leave blanks for the rest; a blank is a note to come back, not a failure. Update it when something changes: a new doctor, a new account, a move.

Keep it safe

Store the binder somewhere the people who'd need it can find it, and tell them where it is. Keep sensitive numbers partial, or note where they're kept rather than writing them out.
