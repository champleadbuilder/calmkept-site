What does a sitter need to know about a child with special needs?

A sitter needs the child's routine, how the child communicates, what helps and what overwhelms, the medical basics (medications, allergies, any action plan), and who to call. Written down, it's the same handover every time, whoever is caring for the child that day.

The handover, in order

  • Who the child is. What brings them joy and calm, and what they'd want a new person to know.
  • Communication. How they communicate, and what common words, signs or behaviors mean.
  • Sensory and regulation. Likes, dislikes, triggers, and what helps them settle.
  • The routine. The day in order: meals, sleep and bedtime, personal care.
  • Medical basics. Current medications and the schedule, allergies, and any medical or seizure action plan the child's care team has written.
  • Contacts. Parents and guardians, the care and therapy team, doctors, and emergency contacts.
  • Equipment and supplies. What the child uses, and where it's kept.

Making it work in practice

  • Keep the first page short: the five things a sitter must know before the door closes.
  • Use the child's own words and routines, not general descriptions.
  • Update it after any change in medication, school or routine.
  • Walk a new sitter through it once, in person, while the child is there.

Any action plan in the binder is the one the child's care team gave the family. The binder holds it so it's easy to find; it doesn't replace it.
