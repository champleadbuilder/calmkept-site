What goes in a medical history binder?

A medical history binder holds one person's health records in one place: the care team, insurance and pharmacy, a full medication list, conditions and allergies, immunizations and screenings, test results and procedures, and the family history a new provider asks about. It's the record you bring to appointments, so your history doesn't depend on memory or on which patient portal holds what.

You and your care

  • You at a glance. Name, date of birth, blood type if you know it, allergies, main conditions, preferred pharmacy and hospital, and a contact to list on forms. It's the page you read from when you fill in any new-patient form.
  • Your care team. Your primary care provider, specialists, dentist, eye doctor, therapists and any mental health provider, with the number that actually reaches each one, plus clinic addresses, patient portal details and patient IDs.
  • Insurance and coverage. Each health, dental, vision and prescription plan, with the member ID, group number and member-services line, or simply where the cards are kept.
  • Pharmacy and refills. Your main and backup pharmacies, and a refill tracker with the next refill date.
  • Medical bills and claims. A plain log of what was billed, what the plan paid and what's still open.

Medications

  • The medication list. For each prescription: name, dose, how often, who prescribed it, what it's for, and when it started.
  • Over-the-counter medications, vitamins and supplements, including anything taken as needed.
  • The medication schedule. What's taken when, across the day and the week.
  • Medication history. What you've taken before, what it was for, when it stopped, and why it changed.

Write what your prescriber ordered. The list is a record; any change comes from your doctor or pharmacist.

Conditions, allergies and prevention

  • Conditions and diagnoses
  • Allergies and reactions
  • Immunizations
  • Screenings and preventive care

Everyday tracking

  • A vitals log for the readings your care team asks you to keep.
  • A symptoms and changes journal, for noting what changed and when, in your own words.

Appointments

  • An appointment log.
  • A before-the-appointment page, to gather what you want to raise.
  • A running list of questions for the next visit.

Tests, procedures and history

  • Test and lab results
  • Surgeries and procedures
  • Hospital and ER visit history
  • Dental, vision and hearing
  • Family medical history

Summaries

  • A one-page medical summary that pulls the essentials together.
  • A one-page summary sheet to hand over.
  • A wallet card for medications and key medical facts.

How to start

Start with the medication list and the allergies page. Those are the details asked for most often, and they change. Then fill in history as you find it: from past paperwork, patient portals and after-visit summaries. You don't have to finish every page in one sitting; a blank is a note to come back. If you're keeping it for one family member rather than yourself, fill it in with them where you can.

Keep it current, and keep it private

Update the binder after every appointment, prescription change or new result. Bring it, or a photo of the medication pages, to each visit, and ask the provider to check it against their record. The binder holds sensitive information, so keep it somewhere secure, and decide who else should know where it is.
