Which important documents should you keep in one place?

Keep the documents someone would need to act for your household, or simply to find things, together and recorded: identification, insurance policies, property and vehicle records, financial account details, tax records, and any legal or care-preference documents you have. Where an original has to stay somewhere else, record where it is.

The documents to gather

  • Identification: birth certificates, passports, Social Security cards, marriage or divorce records.
  • Insurance: health, life, disability, home or renters, auto, and any other policies, with policy numbers and contacts.
  • Property and vehicles: deed or lease, mortgage details, vehicle titles and registrations.
  • Money: bank, investment and retirement account details (institution and where statements are), loans, and recurring bills.
  • Taxes: recent returns and where older ones are kept.
  • Legal and care documents: any you have, such as powers of attorney or care-preference documents, and who holds the originals.
  • Digital life: a record of which accounts exist and where access details are kept. Not the passwords themselves on paper.

Originals vs. a record

You don't have to move every original into one drawer. Some belong in a safe, a safe deposit box or a lawyer's office. What matters is a single record that says what exists and where it is, so the next person knows where to look.

Keep it current

Review the record once a year, and whenever something changes: a new policy, a move, a new account, a closed one.
